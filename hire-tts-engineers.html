Typical engagements

Common shapes for contract work. Useful when you're scoping a brief or checking a quote.

Vendor bake-off

Usually 1–2 weeks

  • Your real scripts run through the TTS APIs you're considering
  • Blind listening tests and latency measured end to end
  • Cost at your expected volume
  • A written recommendation: buy, fine-tune or build

Custom voice

Usually 3–6 weeks

  • Recording plan and consent paperwork for the voice talent
  • Data cleaning and alignment
  • Fine-tuning or voice cloning on your chosen stack
  • Evaluation against the stock voices

Streaming TTS for voice agents

Usually 3–5 weeks

  • Time-to-first-audio under your latency budget
  • Streaming over WebSockets or WebRTC
  • Barge-in and interruption handling with your ASR
  • Load testing at peak concurrency

Pronunciation & text normalization

Usually 2–3 weeks

  • Names, numbers, dates, currencies and acronyms read correctly
  • Custom lexicons and phoneme overrides
  • Regression tests so fixes stay fixed
  • Coverage for each language you support

What to look for in a TTS engineer

What separates someone who has shipped this from someone who has read about it.

  • Model families: modern neural TTS, from VITS-style models to diffusion, flow-matching and codec language models
  • Vocoders and audio codecs, and the quality-versus-latency tradeoffs between them
  • Text front end: text normalization, grapheme-to-phoneme conversion and lexicons
  • Prosody and expressiveness: controlling emphasis, pacing and emotion
  • Evaluation: MOS and blind A/B listening tests, not just automatic metrics
  • Streaming and latency: time-to-first-audio for real-time voice agents
  • Voice cloning responsibly: consent, watermarking and misuse safeguards

FAQ

How much does it cost to hire a text-to-speech engineer?

Posted base pay at speech AI companies has a median range of about $180K-$250K for ML engineering roles and $185K-$320K for research roles as of November 2026, before equity and bonus. Contract projects such as a vendor bake-off or a custom voice are usually scoped as fixed engagements of a few weeks.

Should I build custom TTS or use an API?

Most teams should start with a TTS API and only fine-tune or build when they need a unique brand voice, tighter latency, on-premises deployment, or lower cost at very high volume. A short vendor bake-off with your real scripts usually settles it.

What skills should a TTS engineer have?

Look for experience with modern neural TTS models and vocoders, text normalization and pronunciation, prosody control, listening-test evaluation, and streaming latency for real-time voice agents. For voice cloning, ask how they handle consent and misuse safeguards.

What makes TTS hard to get right in production?

Pronunciation of names, numbers and acronyms, consistent prosody over long passages, and time-to-first-audio when the voice is part of a live conversation. These need regression tests and listening tests, not just a demo.
